# THE WELL

### Pitch

At the bottom of an abandoned well, a dead presence watches the living pass overhead. It cannot leave, cannot speak, and cannot touch the world — except through the things that once belonged to it. By quietly influencing what people see and feel, it can turn an ordinary well into a place of dread, grief, suspicion, or legend.

### Format

- Narrative horror, built to be replayed
- 5–12 minutes, and the length is an outcome of how it is played rather than a
  target: the more the presence makes itself felt, the sooner the living stop
  giving it anything to watch
- Single environment: the bottom of a well, never left
- No objective, no inventory, no dialogue, no puzzle
- No escape; claustrophobia is a primary design goal
- Story driven by sound, by the things in the silt, and by short scenes occurring above

### Player State

- The player is a ghost/presence tied physically to the well.
- The player cannot leave the bottom.
- The player discovers they are dead within the first \~3–5 minutes.
- This is never stated explicitly.
- A corpse and personal belongings provide the main clues.
- Only certain objects are interactable; the player must infer the relationship.
- Personal belongings have specific emotional associations that can be triggered.

### Up There

- People periodically appear around the well.
- Scenes are self-contained events that can be altered by the player.
- The player can affect people through:
  - **Haunting:** noticeable disturbances and supernatural phenomena.
  - **Personal resonance:** interacting with belongings can indirectly alter a person's emotional state.
- These effects are discovered through observation rather than explained as explicit mechanics.
- Emotional states affect how people subsequently perceive and treat the well/player.
- Earlier scenes can therefore alter later scenes and story paths.

### Story Structure

- No explicit player objective.
- The player observes, experiments, and gradually understands the rules.
- Story paths emerge from accumulated reactions Up There.
- Broad possible directions include the well becoming:
  - feared as haunted
  - investigated as a mystery
  - associated with guilt or tragedy
  - treated as a dangerous place to be sealed or destroyed
- The exact truth of the player's death and the well's history remains partly ambiguous.
- A living person being thrown into the well is a major canon event, occurring mid-to-late game depending on the path.
- The player can influence this person's emotional state and circumstances indirectly, but cannot directly interact with them.
- The consequences of the victim's presence are a major later-game branch.

### Core Design Principle

**The player cannot leave the well, but they can change what the living believe happened there.**
