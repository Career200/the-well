# Beat zero

The first ninety seconds. The presence is alone: no voices, no shadow across the
light, nobody to affect. It ends when the first light crosses the coin of sky
and the run begins.

It teaches four things and states none of them:

```
1  you can act, and acting costs
2  stillness gives it back
3  the things down here do not give it back
4  a scene runs in beats, and a beat is when you can act
```

Four is the sleeper. Beat zero runs on the same beat-and-window rhythm every
scene uses, so when the first person arrives the player already has the tempo.

---

## Nothing is unlocked

There is no unlock logic and no tutorial gating. All three stances are available
from the first second; two of them simply have nothing to act on yet.

- **Pressing** is the only input with a visible consequence in the dark, so it
  is the one the player finds first.
- **Stillness** is illegible until something has been spent. It becomes readable
  the moment the water is no longer glass.
- **Holding** is impossible until something has been seen well enough to take
  up.

That ordering is a property of the world, not a script. A player who sits
perfectly still for the whole phase still reaches the end, having learned less,
and the light crosses anyway.

## Three movements

Each is a handful of short beats. Seedship pace — act and move — not a
meditation.

**I · the dark.** Sensation with no place and no body attached to it. Pressing
does something: the water answers, because it is the only thing here that does.
The ambient subjects resolve in fixed order as the phase runs — **the cold, the
water, the walls, the sky, the silt** — and they resolve whether or not the
player acts.

**II · the settling.** Presence is down. The water will not go back to glass at
once, and the player has nothing to do but wait, which is the lesson. This is
where stillness stops being the absence of input and becomes a move.

**III · the things.** Pressing disturbs the silt and the silt gives something
up. **Two of the four belongings, chosen at random, are reachable in beat
zero** — they go glimpse → plain, and holding them becomes possible. The other
two are not found here; they belong to the run.

Two of four is deliberate: the player leaves the dark with an incomplete
inventory they do not know is incomplete, and two runs open differently.

## What it spends

Real resource, small enough to be cheap and large enough to be noticed.

- Pressing spends presence, which returns.
- Holding spends that belonging, permanently, and beat zero is the only place in
  the game where learning that costs almost nothing — there is nobody above to
  have wasted it on.

The player enters the run slightly down on both. That is correct.

## Exhaustion

Pressing until there is nothing left is its own outcome, not a failure. It is
the one moment where both economies are visible at once, because one of them is
empty and the others are not.

```
the water   will not settle at all, and stops reflecting anything
the things  are still warm, and warmth is now obviously a different substance
            from whatever has just run out
```

The reveal is the relationship: **the water is the presence, and the belongings
are not.** A player who burns out in the first minute learns the economy faster
than a careful one and pays for it in a phase where the payment does not matter.

Existing line, and it lands here rather than anywhere else:

> The water is going nowhere and going there fast. There is nothing of you left
> in it.

## Ending

A soft floor and a hard ceiling. The light does not cross before the ambient
five have resolved, and it crosses regardless after the phase's turn limit
whatever state the player is in. Beat zero can be under-played; it cannot be
failed and it cannot be blocked.

## Content

Most of it already exists in `descriptions/below.md`, which has *veiled* and
*plain* for all nine subjects and glimpses for the four belongings.

**Names come later.** Beat zero delivers glimpse and plain only; the *named*
tier is earned during the run, and the `named` blocks in the descriptions are
late-game payoff rather than opening material.

Still to write, and it is small:

```
the opening dark      sensation before any subject resolves      3–4 blocks
movement transitions  I→II→III, felt rather than announced       3 blocks
exhaustion            the outcome above                           2 blocks
the light crossing    the end, and the run beginning              1 block
```

Roughly ten blocks, ~400 words.

## What building it should tell us

The reason to build this before anything else is that it answers four questions
cheaply, and every one of them is load-bearing for the rest of the game.

1. Does discovery-by-stance work with no instruction at all?
2. Does the tier text read as escalation, or as the same thing said twice?
3. How long does it actually take, in seconds, against the 90 assumed here?
4. Does the player understand that holding cost them something they cannot get
   back?

If four is a no, the economy needs rework before any more prose is written.
