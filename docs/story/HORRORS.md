# The horrors

Every other document here describes the machine. This one describes what the
machine is for, and it exists because the first four deliverables could be read
end to end without once being frightening. A cast pool, a fact graph, an
escalation curve — a plot machine, correctly specified, and sterile.

The one-sentence version: **the player's progress is measured in what has been
taken from them, and three institutions are coming to take it — fed by a supply
that never runs out.**

---

## 1. What was missing

`FACTS.md` has forty facts and not one of them is *someone died of the water you
are lying in*. `CAST.md` has twelve people and none of them can be lost. The
belongings can be spent, voluntarily, by a player who understands the economy —
and that is the entire inventory of things that can go wrong for the presence.

Meanwhile the four beliefs — `haunted`, `mystery`, `tragedy`, `danger` — are
abstract labels with no content behind them. They move, they gate the coda, and
they mean nothing on the way. That is where the spice was supposed to live and
nothing was put there.

## 2. The law

> **Both ends of every axis hurt.**

Right now each meter has one bad end and one safe one, and the safe end is where
a careful player parks. Naming the floor horror for each is most of the fix:

| axis      | ceiling                                              | floor                                                   |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| attention | the crowd, the hearing, the boards                   | **you are forgotten, and being forgotten takes things** |
| dread     | the village closes and the run ends                  | nothing happens, forever, and you watch                 |
| lucidity  | you know exactly who you were and can say none of it | you never find out                                      |
| charge    | you spent it and it does not come back               | you saved it and the water took it anyway               |
| beliefs   | whichever one wins comes for somebody                | —                                                       |

The floor of attention is the one that was actively mis-written. `FACTS.md`
framed `well.disused` as the gentle path. It is the worst one — see §7.

## 3. The roads

**Three roads and a supply.** Each road is an institution with an escalation the
player cannot call off. They share most of the deck and diverge at the end,
which is the only way three horrors fit inside twenty situations.

| | the thing that cannot be stopped |
| --- | --- |
| **the cult** | devotion, and no input de-escalates it |
| **the investigation** | the one person asking properly is removed |
| **the sealing** | the boards, and then the forgetting |
| *the sickness* | *a supply, not a road — §5. People go, and the three roads are what consume it* |

**All of it runs at once, from turn one.** A road is not a belief's ending and
not a thing that gets armed: it is a live track with its own pressure, and the
run is about whichever ramps hardest. The others sit at whatever stage they
reached and the coda reads them too. Beliefs are how loud each road has got —
what the player overhears — never a selector. `MECHANICS.md` §4 has the
pressure model and what each road feeds and damps in the others.

The reason none of them can be finished is one sentence: **there is a body in
the water and nobody can take it out.** Every road is an attempt to deal with
that, every attempt fails, and every failure is another road's fuel.

### the cult

`offerings.left` is already the seed and it was written without knowing what it
was for. The chain: something is left at the rim → someone comes back to see
whether it was taken → a vigil → a second person, then more → words that are not
Orla's words → and eventually a thing that is not an object.

The horror is entirely mechanical. **Pressing is read as answering.** Not
pressing is read as displeasure, which escalates the response. There is no input
that means *stop*, because the presence has two levers and neither of them
is a sentence. The player builds a god by accident, in four beats, and then
watches the congregation work out what a god wants.

This is the road where the brief's `unguarded` rule pays its best dividend: a
person who believes they are alone at the rim of a well they worship is the most
frightening cast in the game.

### the investigation

Someone comes who asks properly, writes things down, and is not from here. Three
ends, and the player influences which:

- **bought** — Anselm has money or standing enough, and the questions stop mid-
  sentence. The player watches a man decide it is not worth it.
- **frightened off** — the presence did this. The one person who might have said
  the name out loud leaves because of the player, and the player will work that
  out afterwards.
- **too far** — he finds something, and the village removes him.

All three are the same lesson: **progress is punished, and the player is often
the one who punished it.** This is the road that makes helping feel worse than
haunting.

### the sealing

Boards, hurdles, a rule about children, and then the slow part.

**Its terminal is the forgetting**, deliberately. The village stops needing to
close the hole because it stops thinking about the hole, and §7 is what happens
to the presence next. It is the only road whose ending is an absence rather than
an event, and that is not a defect — it is the same ending a run arrives at when
nothing ramped at all, reached on purpose instead of by default. One ending, two
doors: the village that closed it, and the village that never decided there was
anything to close.

It is also the road every other road feeds. Whatever happens up there, the
village's generic answer to an incident is to close the hole a little further —
so a busy run trends here regardless of what it was busy *with*.

## 4. The throwing is where they all arrive

The canon event stays exactly canon and stops needing to be motivated in the
abstract. Every road reaches somebody going into the well, for irreconcilable
reasons:

```
the cult           it is given
the investigation  it is silenced
the sealing        it is disposed of, practically, because the hole is there
```

And once the sickness has been named, any of the three can reach it by a fourth
route: **it is blamed.** The one who poisoned the water is whoever the loudest
road had already decided to be suspicious of.

*Who is thrown* is not an authoring choice and not a belief threshold: it is
**whichever road got there first**, and the casting follows from that. Silenced
and blamed both take the one who came asking; given and disposed of will take
him too if he is the only outsider to hand. That is why the stranger is no
longer a placeholder — he and the investigator are one record, and the player
has watched him work.

## 5. The sickness

**It is not a road. It is a supply**, and the three roads are what consume it.
People go; the cult appeases it, the investigation asks about it, the sealing
closes the hole it came out of.

**Why it never stops.** There is a body in the water. The village draws from
that water because it must, and nobody can take the body out — not them, not the
presence. The supply therefore runs on every run, at a trickle, needing no
player input and admitting no interruption.

**Why it usually stays small.** A trickle is one household in a bad year. The
village has language for that already and does not need a word for it.

**What turns it into a road.** Being named. `water.suspected`, chalked doors,
the cart going through twice in a week. Once named it gains steps of its own —
who is still drawing here, who has stopped, who cannot afford to stop — and from
then on it ramps like anything else. Until named it has no steps at all, and
that is the whole of the distinction.

**What the presence sees of it.** Never the sick, who stop coming. Only the
buckets that go up, and which hands are on the rope, and the gaps where a person
used to be in the rota. The village works it out slowly, or wrongly, or never.

And here the two levers turn over:

> **Pressing becomes mercy.** The only way to stop someone drinking from this
> well is to make the well frightening. Haunting saves lives, and it costs the
> run its length, its warmth and its registers.

The careful, watch-everything playstyle is the one that lets them keep drinking.

**The question it leaves open.** The death must stay partly ambiguous, and this
supplies the version of that worth having:

> *Did I have it. Did I come down here on purpose. Was that a mercy, or was that
> the thing that put it in the water.*

The evidence cuts both ways and is not added to: a coat torn at the shoulder in
a way the fall does not explain, a knife bedded to the hilt as though it went in
after you did, and a body in the village's water. A man who went into a well to
keep himself out of the village and became the source is the same set of objects
as a man who was put there.

**What it costs to build.** The cast becomes mortal. People leave the pool
permanently, casting and recurrence have to survive it, and `CAST.md`'s coverage
table becomes a depletion table. It also gives the pool a reason to be twelve
rather than a comfort.

**Vocabulary.** The word is still never used. A village with a sickness develops
its own language for it and that language is free atmosphere: *the laying-out*,
*the cart*, *he went in the night*, *the house is shut*, *they have chalked the
door*. Orla was written as the person who says the words when someone is put in
the ground, before any of this existed. She is the officiant of this road and
she was already in the pool.

## 6. Loss is the progress bar

The player's only irreversible resource is currently spent by choice. Make the
world able to take it and the whole thing acquires teeth.

**Water level is an event affordance, not weather.** `water.high` / `water.low`
were filed under ambient. They are the mechanism by which the well takes things
back.

```
water.high   the coat lifts, drifts, and settles further away each time. Three
             warnings, then it is out of reach and it is not coming back
water.low    the silt shows — and what is in the silt can be seen from up there
```

**Things can be taken Up There.** `well.sounded`, a hook, a low water, a bucket
that comes up heavy: the ring goes to the village. Every consequence of that is
good. The living get `ring.recognised` and a name to say. The presence loses a
lever it was saving. The single most informative object in the game becomes the
one the player can no longer use, and the information and the capability are
traded against each other in the same beat.

The rules that keep this from feeling arbitrary:

- **Warned, not random.** Three escalating signs before anything is lost. The
  player who was paying attention could have spent it; the loss is their
  hesitation, not a die roll.
- **Loss is never silent.** It prints, from below, in full.
- **Nothing is ever recovered.** No object comes back, at any tier, on any road.
- **A spent object and a stolen object read differently.** Spent is cold. Taken
  is *absent*, and the prose keeps going to where it was.

## 7. Deterioration, and why being forgotten is the worst ending

The design treated the quiet path as the merciful one. It should be the one that
frightens people into never playing it again.

**Attention has a floor and nothing was written for it.** When nobody comes, for
long enough, the presence thins. Concretely, and all of it taken from the player
rather than described to them:

- **Affordances go.** The scene-local things the presence could do stop being
  available, one at a time, with no announcement.
- **Recovery slows.** Stillness returns less. The water takes longer to settle
  and eventually does not.
- **The belongings go quiet** — not spent, and not cold. Unreachable, because
  there is nobody up there for them to reach.

**And the prose regresses.** This is the mechanism the whole document was
looking for, and it costs nothing, because it is already written:

> **Deterioration is lucidity running backwards.** `named` → `plain` → `veiled`,
> through the same three tiers already authored for eighteen subjects. *Your
> ring, she gave it back* becomes *a ring, it does not fit anything down here*
> becomes *the small hard thing, cold, and it will not warm.*

Nothing new to write, and the effect is savage: the player re-reads text they
have already earned, in the wrong direction, and understands exactly what is
being subtracted because they remember what used to be there.

Literal erosion of the text — letters and words going — is held in reserve for
the last minute and the coda, where it is earned and cannot be mistaken for a
gimmick. Under rules: never illegible; the specific goes before the general;
**names go first**, then objects, then places, and the last thing standing is a
verb with nothing attached to it. However, this is up to consideration from a
technical standpoint, and stealing letters is a good scare factor and indication
of wrongness to the player earlier on.

The ending that road arrives at is the one the critique named, and it is the
best ending in the game. "**How is this still awake?**" it asks about the player.

## 8. What it costs

Four institutions do not fit in twenty situations as four plots. They fit as
**shared middle, divergent end**:

```
12  shared, played differently by belief — water, the rim, the children,
    the pair talking, the hearing, the sounding, the dog
 8  road-specific, two per belief, all late
──
20  and the throwing is one situation with four castings
```

Corpus impact, against the ~21,000 in `CURVE.md` §9: the shared twelve absorb
belief variation in their outcome tables rather than in new prose; the eight
road situations are already inside the count; the sickness adds an illness track
across the cast and roughly **+1,500** for the vocabulary of a village burying
people. Call it **~22,500**, and the coda gets more valuable per word, not less,
because there are now four genuinely different things to have caused.

## 9. What breaks

Honest list, because three of these are real.

- **The cast becomes mortal**, and casting, `prefer`, recurrence arcs and the
  coverage table all have to survive people being gone. This is the largest
  mechanical consequence in the document.
- **The premise moves on the runs where it ramps.** `PITCH.md` does not mention a
  sickness, and where the road gets going it is not a subplot — it is the
  weather over the whole run. Left at a trickle it is one unlucky household,
  which is a very different game, and the machine decides which without being
  asked.
- **The budget was already over.** `FACTS.md` Appendix B listed twenty-plus
  candidates against a ceiling of twenty before any of this. The roads make the
  pruning harder, not easier, and something has to be cut on purpose rather than
  by running out of time.
- **Ambiguity comes under pressure.** Four institutions all want an explanation
  at the end, and the rule is that the death is never resolved. The roads must
  each arrive at a *verdict the village reached*, never at a fact the game
  confirms — which is the readout law extended to the endings.
- **Cruelty needs a floor.** Children in the pool, a sickness, and a cult that
  eventually wants a person is a combination that can go somewhere the register
  will not survive. The dog rule holds throughout: strange means wrong, never
  lurid. What is done to people happens off the coin of sky, arrives as sound,
  and is never described with relish.
