# Brief — Below, and the first minute

*Deliberately self-contained. It refers to no other document and needs none.
Everything required to do this work is on this page.*

---

## The place

At the bottom of an abandoned well there is a presence. It cannot leave, cannot
speak, cannot touch anything, and nobody living can perceive it. It has been
here long enough that it has stopped counting, and it does not yet know what it
is. The player is this presence.

Down here with it: water, silt, stone, cold, a coin of sky a long way up, and
four things that used to belong to somebody. One of them is on a hand.

The presence never learns what it is by being told. It learns by looking at
things until the things stop being evasive.

**The word is never used.** Not once, in any register, at any tier. The prose
simply stops being careful about what it is describing.

## The first minute

Before anyone comes to the well, the presence is alone with all of the above.
No voices, no shadow across the light, nothing to affect. This is the whole of
what you are writing for.

There are three things the presence can do, and there will never be more:

```
still      doing nothing. It returns something to the presence
press      pushing at the world. It spends that something
hold       taking up one of the four things. It spends that thing, and what it
           spends does not come back. Ever.
```

The surface of the water shows how much the presence has left. Full and unspent,
it is glass. Spent, it will not settle. Existing lines, which are the register
to match:

> The water is glass. Nothing moves on it at all.
> The water is almost still. A ring goes out from the wall and dies.
> The water turns slowly against the stone, the way it does after something.
> The water will not settle. It keeps finding the wall.

A thing taken up goes *warm · cooling · nearly cold · cold, and staying cold*,
and the last of those is permanent. That is the most important fact in the game
and no sentence anywhere is allowed to say it out loud.

The minute ends when the first light crosses the coin of sky. It ends whether or
not the presence has done anything at all — **so most of what you write will be
read by someone sitting still, and it has to be worth their time.**

## The three tiers

Everything down here is written three times, at three depths of self-knowledge.
The presence moves through them by looking. This is the shape:

```
veiled   sensation. No names, no certainty
         the small hard thing. cold, and it will not warm.

plain    the thing named, its history not yet claimed
         a ring. it does not fit anything down here.

named    yours. Said so
         your ring. she gave it back and you carried it in your pocket ever
         since.

extra    only at named — one more beat that exists at no other tier
```

Two properties matter more than the wording.

**There is more text at the higher tiers, not merely different text.** Awareness
is felt as the world getting louder, not clearer. Roughly: one or two sentences
veiled, two or three plain, three or four named plus the extra beat.

**The four belongings run one tier ahead of the stone and the water.** They are
the surface where the presence knows itself best, so they arrive at *named*
before the walls do.

## The nine subjects

Five that are the well:

- **the water** — it is everywhere, it is what the presence is mostly made of
  now, and it is the one thing that answers
- **the cold** — not weather. A property of the place, and of being here
- **the walls** — wet stone, courses of it, close enough to touch on every side
- **the sky** — a coin of light, very high, the only thing that changes on its
  own
- **the silt** — what everything has settled into. Soft, deep, and it holds
  what is put in it

Four that are the belongings. What they are, as raw material:

- **a brass ring**, green at the edges. It is on a finger. The finger is on a
  hand in the silt, palm up, and the presence knows the shape of that hand
  better than it knows its own name
- **a tin whistle**, flattened on one side where a boot came down. Somebody
  carried it a long way for someone who never got it
- **a short knife**, bedded in the silt to the hilt as though it went in after
  you did. The handle is wrapped in cord, and the presence's hands knew how to
  wrap cord like that
- **a coat**, spread and heavy with water, torn at the shoulder in a way the
  fall does not explain. You keep expecting it to be cold and it is not, and
  that is the wrong thing about it

Each belonging also gets a **glimpse** — what it is before it has been looked
at properly. One line. Existing examples: *a small brightness in the silt* ·
*something with a hole in it* · *a straight line where nothing should be
straight* · *a dark spread against the dark*.

## Rules

**Everything is inside the presence's senses.** It can hear what a person at the
bottom of a well hears, and see what is visible through a coin of sky. It cannot
follow a bucket up the shaft. It cannot know what is happening on the path, in a
house, or behind anyone's face. If a sentence requires the presence to have gone
somewhere, it is wrong, and this is the failure to watch for above all others.

**Nothing explains anything.** No sentence describes what the player can do, or
what a stance costs, or what will happen if they wait. Things respond; that is
the whole of the teaching.

**Nothing is confirmed.** The reader may work out what happened here. The text
never says. Two readings must survive every line at *named*: that this was done
to the presence, and that the presence did it.

**Strange means wrong, never funny**, and never lurid. Nothing shouts. More
specific is worse; louder is not.

**No wit.** Dryness is a kind of composure and composure is a kind of warmth. A
sentence that turns at the end — that lands a small clever observation — is
right once in a while at *veiled* and wrong at *named*, where there is no
distance left. Let the voice lose its irony as it goes down the tiers.

**Sound before sight** wherever both are available.

## What to hand back

For each of the nine subjects, in this shape:

```
### the water

veiled   …
plain    …
named    …
extra    …          (only where it earns one — expect about five of nine)
```

Plus one **glimpse** line for each of the four belongings.

**About 36 blocks and about 1,400 words in total. No single block over sixty
words.** This is a hard ceiling, not a target to grow into. A subject that wants
more room is a subject that has started explaining.

Write it once, straight through. Then make exactly one pass, checking only these
five things, and change nothing else:

1. Is every line inside what the presence can hear or see from the bottom of a
   well?
2. Does the word appear anywhere? It must not.
3. Is there more text at *named* than at *veiled* for every subject?
4. Does any sentence explain a mechanic?
5. Does any sentence turn at the end to be clever?

If you needed something this page does not give you, list it at the end in three
lines or fewer. Do not invent it and do not go looking for it.
