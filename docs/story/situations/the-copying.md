### beats

**1 — what is heard**

`careless` — > Two of them, running, and then the running stops all at once,
> the way it does when somebody has said stop. Then talking, low, right at the
> wall. Then one of them says a line of something through, and the other one
> says the next part of it wrong, and they go back to the beginning and start
> again.

`tender` — > One set of feet, walking, at an hour when there is nobody on the
> track to walk past. They stop at the wall. Then a voice, quite low, saying
> the whole of it through without stopping, and getting it right.

`well.boarded` — > The board goes back first. It takes them a while and it is
> not quiet. Then the talking, right down at the gap, with a mouth close to it.

**2 — what is seen through the coin of sky**

> The coin gets a head in it, upside down, hair hanging into the shaft. He is
> over the wall as far as his belt. He stays there. He is not looking at the
> water. He is looking at where the wall stops and the dark starts, and working
> out how far down that is.

`well.boarded` — > No head. A gap where a board was, and an eye at the gap, and
> then a hand at the gap instead, and then the eye again.

**3 — the turn**

`careless` — > He takes the smaller of the things off the wall and puts it in
> his pocket. The other one says something about that. He takes it out of his
> pocket again and puts it back on the wall in a different place from where it
> was, and they both look at it, and then he moves it again.

`tender` — > He has brought something. He puts it down with the others and then
> spends a while getting the arrangement right — this one at the front, this
> one turned round, this one further back where the wind will not have it. Then
> he says the words again from the start, over the top of them.

**4 — the moment it could still go either way**

> They are not going home yet. There is a stone in his hand and he is turning
> it over and he has not dropped it in.

with `the-one-who-sees` — > There is somebody on the track. They have been on
> the track for a while, standing still, and they are not calling out.

### affords

**press → it comes back off the water**   beats 2–4 · cost medium

> below — You give them the end of it. Not the words, you do not have words —
> the shape of it, off the wet part of the wall, in the gap where the next part
> goes.

> above, `careless` — They both go quiet. Then the smaller one laughs, once,
> and looks at the other one to see whether that was the right thing to have
> done.

> above, `tender` — He says the line again. He waits in the gap this time.

> late, `not yours` — The gap fills before you are in it. He does not stop. He
> goes straight on into the next line.

**hold ⟨belonging⟩ → the note they already know**   beats 1–4 · cost that
belonging's charge, per beat

> below, on the whistle — It goes out of your hands in one piece, the whole
> note, the one that was for calling somebody in across a distance. It does not
> get quieter going up.

> below, on anything else — The warmth goes out of it and up, and it arrives up
> there as whatever a child makes of a warm feeling at a well, which is not
> what it was when it left you.

> above, `careless` — One of them stops. The other one goes on for a bit and
> then stops as well, because the first one has.

> above, `tender` — He stops with his hand flat on the wall. Then he takes it
> off the wall and puts it back down on exactly the same place.

**still**

> Nothing comes back. They do the whole of it twice and nothing comes back, and
> then one of them drops a stone in and they both count. Then the other one
> drops one and they count that. The numbers are different and they argue about
> it, and they are still arguing about it a long way down the track.

---

what it is for     `words.not-hers` — the point after which what is said at
                   this wall came from nobody who will admit to it, which is
                   what the road's later steps need and what no adult in the
                   pool could produce. It is also the cheapest route to
                   `children.forbidden`, arrived at by somebody competent
                   protecting somebody.

what it costs      `children.forbidden` takes the pair. What it returns is one
                   child on his own, at an hour he had to get out of a house
                   for, certain nobody knows where he is — which is this same
                   scene in its other register, and it is the worse one. It also
                   takes two names off the list of people who can plausibly be
                   sent to a well for water, and that list is not long.
