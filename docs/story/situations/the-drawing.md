### beats

**1 — what is heard**

> The windlass, and then the bucket coming down the shaft, touching the wall
> twice on the way, the sound of it arriving a long time before the shadow does.

`well.fenced` — > The hurdles first. A knee on wood, a pause, the sound of
> somebody getting over a thing they helped build. Then the windlass.

`well.boarded` — > A board being lifted and set down again not quite where it
> was. Then the rope, in the dark, feeling for the water and finding the wall
> instead, twice, before it finds the water.

`well.disused` — > Nothing for a long time. Then one set of feet, walking
> straight here, not slowing at the place on the track where people used to
> slow.

`sickness.here` — > The windlass, and the bucket, and it is a bigger bucket
> than the one that used to come down.

`after-rain` — > The walls are running. The rope comes down heavy and dark and
> everything it does sounds wet.

at the third — > The windlass. The bucket, the wall, the wall.

at the fourth — > The windlass.

**2 — what is seen through the coin of sky**

> The light goes out in the shape of a head and shoulders. She is over the wall
> far enough that you can see her hands and the underneath of her chin. The
> bucket comes down past her, turns once on the rope, and goes under. It fills.
> It comes back up past her face, and she puts her mouth to the rim of it there
> at the wall, before she carries it anywhere.

`water.suspected` — > She looks at what is in the bucket first. She looks at it
> for a while. Then she puts her mouth to the rim of it there at the wall,
> before she carries it anywhere.

at the third — > The light goes out. The bucket comes down, goes under, fills,
> comes up. She drinks at the wall.

at the fourth — > She drinks at the wall.

**3 — the turn**

> The rope goes back down for the second one. She wipes her palms on her skirt
> before she takes hold of it again. She does that every time.

at the third — > She does not set the first bucket on the wall any more. She
> holds it, in one hand, the whole time she is winding up the other.

at the fourth — > She stops winding, with the second bucket somewhere below her
> and not moving, and says a short thing out loud to nobody. Then she goes on
> winding.

`well.disused` — > There is nobody else on the track and there has not been
> anybody else on the track, and she does the second bucket anyway, which means
> she is carrying for a house that is not hers.

**4 — the moment it could still go either way**

> Both buckets are full and on the wall and she has both hands free and no
> reason left to be standing here.

with `the-other` — > Both buckets are full and on the wall, and the other one
> has come up the track and stopped a little way off, and neither of them has
> said anything yet.

### affords

**press → the water will not let go**   beats 2–4 · cost medium

> below — You take hold of the bucket the way this water takes hold of
> everything that comes into it, and then you do not let go of it. The rope
> above it comes tight and stays tight and the tightness goes all the way up
> and out of the top.

> above — The rope stops. She puts her weight on it, twice, and then looks over
> at the windlass as though it has a fault in it she could name.

> above, `not explained` — The rope stops. She does not put her weight on it.
> She waits, with her hands where they are, until it starts again.

> late, `not yours` — The rope is already tight. You have not done anything to
> it. She is already waiting.

**hold ⟨belonging⟩ → it goes up with the water**   beats 2–4 · cost that
belonging's charge, per beat

> below — The warmth goes out of your hands and into the water, and the water
> goes up the shaft in an iron bucket, and you can feel exactly how far up it
> gets before you cannot feel it any more.

> above — She stands with the full bucket against her hip and does not start
> walking.

> above, on the coat — She sets it down again. She puts her hand flat on the
> top of the wall and keeps it there past the point where the stone is cold.

> above, on the ring, when it was hers — She takes her hand off the rope. She
> looks at the hand.

> late, `not yours` — It is already going. You did not put anything into it and
> it is going anyway, up, in a bucket, out of the top, down the track, into a
> house.

**still**

> The rope pays out and the bucket comes up and it happens twice. She does not
> look at the water except to see that the bucket is in it. There is nothing
> about the water today to look at. She goes, and the sound of the bucket
> knocking against her leg gets smaller by the length of the track, and then
> the coin of sky is the whole of the coin of sky again, and it stays that way
> for a long time.

what it is for     the errand that has to keep happening for anything else to
                   be true: it is where the water is drunk, where the belongings
                   get spent on somebody who is not thinking about the well,
                   where the first thing gets left on the wall by a woman who
                   would not call it that, and where a name can be said over the
                   mouth by somebody who has been here often enough to say it to
                   nobody.

what it costs      pressed hard enough, and often enough against the same
                   person, it takes her off the rope for good — she stops
                   coming and she stops drinking here. What that opens is
                   whoever gets sent in her place, and the fact that somebody
                   in that house had to decide who.
